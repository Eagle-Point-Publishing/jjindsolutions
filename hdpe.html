Why mines run on poly

The pipe that handles what mines move

Tailings, slurry, and aggressive process water eat steel pipe from the inside out. Fused HDPE behaves differently: the welded joint is as strong as the pipe itself, so a properly fused line runs leak-free, shrugs off corrosion and abrasion, and flexes with ground movement instead of cracking at a coupling.

The catch is in that word "properly." Poly welding is a discipline. Heat, pressure, and cool-down time make or break a butt fusion joint, and a bad one hides until it fails under load. That's why the crew matters more than the pipe.

  • Butt fusion joints made to ASTM F2620 procedures
  • Fusion records kept per joint, so your engineers can audit the line
  • Steel-to-HDPE transitions and flange connections by the same crew
  • Welding, fabrication, and millwright support on the same mobilization

Quality you can hand to your engineer

Every joint to the standard, every joint on record

A fused line is only as good as its worst joint, so we don't treat fusion quality as a matter of trust. We treat it as a matter of records.

Straight answers

HDPE questions we hear

What sizes and pressures do you handle?

Scope varies by project, so bring us the pipe spec and duty. Fusion procedures follow ASTM F2620, and a coordinator will confirm equipment fit for your diameter and pressure class on the first call.

Can you work on an active mine site?

Yes. Our crews carry MSHA and industrial facility experience, we're an ISNetworld member contractor, and we badge in under your safety program. Remote and camp-based work is normal for us.

Why fused HDPE instead of steel or gasketed pipe?

For abrasive and corrosive service, poly usually wins on lifetime cost: no corrosion, high abrasion tolerance, and fused joints with no gaskets to weep or couplings to work loose. Steel still has its place, which is why our crews do the transitions between the two.

Do you handle the whole line or just the fusion?

Both. We can run fusion support for your installation crew, or take the full scope: fusion, placement, transitions to steel, tie-ins, and pressure testing, with welding and fabrication support from the same team.

How do we know the joints are good?

Every joint is fused to ASTM F2620 procedures and logged, and the line is pressure-tested before handoff. You get the records, not just our word.

How fast can a crew mobilize?

We're mobile-first out of Twin Falls with crews that travel across Idaho and Alaska. Call with the scope and a coordinator will give you a straight answer on timing for your site.
